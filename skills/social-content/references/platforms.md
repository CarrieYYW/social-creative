# Platform Strategy Options

Use this guide to adapt an idea to a destination, not to predict reach. Choose channels, formats, cadence, and timing from the active audience, objective, source material, production capacity, and actual account results. These are creative options, not ranked formats, demographic guarantees, or algorithm rules.

## LinkedIn

For a professional audience, consider an insight tied to a concrete example, a sourced argument, a useful document/carousel, or an authentic account of work. Make the opening clear, preserve qualifications, and use paragraph structure that supports reading. A personal story requires actual supplied experience.

Decide where a link belongs from the task and reader journey; do not automatically hide a necessary link in comments to chase unverified reach claims. Choose post length from the idea and current destination limits. Evaluate relevant audience responses or attributable actions rather than assuming comments always outweigh other outcomes.

## Twitter/X

Consider a concise observation, a coherent thread, a visual explanation, or timely commentary when it fits the audience. Give each thread segment a role and enough context while maintaining progression. Use quote/repost commentary when it adds something useful and is within the user's requested work.

Choose length and thread count from the message. Do not assume a universal character sweet spot, paid account boost, posting quota, or early engagement window. Check current account-tier limits when preparing delivery.

## Instagram

Choose a static quote, carousel, reel, or story from what the message needs. A strong sentence may work as typography alone; a sequence can explain relationships; a video can demonstrate change, convey delivery, or develop a thought over time.

Keep important content legible in the actual destination and preview crops. Use a carousel length that completes the reading arc, and a video pace that supports understanding. Compare formats using relevant account results; do not claim a fixed reel reach multiplier or infer that static posts are inherently inferior. Use interactive elements when they help the audience, rather than requiring every feature.

## TikTok

Consider demonstration, explanation, narrative, or a response to a real audience question. Establish the subject or value clearly and fulfill the promise. Match polish, pacing, and performance to the brand and idea; quiet delivery and carefully produced work are valid options.

Trends, sounds, and participatory formats are optional. Check relevance, rights, and actual availability before building around them. Verify the requested upload surface rather than assuming a universal duration cap or aspect ratio. Do not describe cadence as training the algorithm.

## Facebook

For a relevant audience or community, consider a useful explanation, native video, local story/event, discussion prompt, or adapted visual post. Give the reader enough context and an appropriate next action. Use links when they serve the intended task; do not promise a reach benefit from removing them.

Adapt cross-posted material for the destination's presentation and audience. Community participation, messages, live posts, and scheduled publication require the user's explicit instructions; a proposed engagement plan is not execution authorization.

## Plan, test, and verify

Pick a feasible starting cadence with room to review and respond. Use audience/account data to choose observation windows and posting-time experiments; generic clock times are not evidence for this audience. Do not increase volume merely because reach is low.

Compare paid and organic work separately and define the primary outcome and baseline using [Creative Testing and Learning](creative-testing.md). Keep observations distinct from causal explanations. Use [Platform Delivery Verification](platform-limits.md) for current technical requirements; creative guidance does not establish uploader compatibility.
