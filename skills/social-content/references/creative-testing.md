# Creative Testing and Learning

Use when planning creative variants, interpreting supplied analytics, or deciding what to improve next. A one-off caption or ordinary production request does not require an experiment.

## Define the decision

Choose the audience, intended outcome, and question the result will inform. Check the account's existing assets and actual outcomes before proposing new production. Reuse strong material when it fits the present goal; preserve the original and its source context.

Select a primary metric that represents the goal and is available in the supplied data. Define its numerator and denominator: for example, saves divided by reached accounts, rather than an undefined engagement rate. Retention, shares, qualified follows, clicks, or attributable actions answer different questions. Track secondary metrics when needed to catch a misleading improvement, such as a stronger opening with a weaker completed-view or conversion result.

Compare like with like where possible: account, audience, paid/organic distribution, format, duration, objective, and elapsed time since publication. Use a comparable account baseline, such as a cohort median, and explain exclusions. Do not treat an overall account average or a viral outlier as a fair baseline automatically.

## Design the comparison

Record only the fields relevant to the decision:

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | What change might improve which outcome, and why |
| Control and variant | Actual asset/version identifiers and the meaningful difference |
| Fixed elements | Body copy/edit, destination, cover, targeting, budget, or other relevant controls |
| Measurement | Primary metric definition, source, baseline, observation window, and review point |
| Exposure | Reached accounts, impressions, views, spend, or sample information available |
| Limitations | Audience/timing differences, missing data, paid promotion, or other confounders |
| Result and decision | Observed numbers, uncertainty, next action, and what remains untested |

For an opening test, change a meaningful mechanism, such as a direct takeaway versus a question. Keep the body edit stable. If testing the first frame, spoken opening, and display text together, call it an opening-package test; the result cannot isolate each component. A carousel cover test should preserve the inner sequence unless the whole structure is the hypothesis. Do not imply that producing variants authorizes publishing them.

Use valid randomized platform experiments when available and authorized. Separate organic posts usually differ in exposure and timing; report those comparisons as directional. Predefine a review window appropriate to distribution and capacity. Do not impose a universal sample size, significance threshold, daily volume, or winner cutoff; mark insufficient evidence as inconclusive.

## Read results without inventing causes

Separate observations, explanations, and next tests. A retention drop identifies a point to inspect against the actual edit; it does not establish that a caption, pause, or CTA caused viewers to leave. Check alternative explanations before recommending a revision.

Do not substitute predicted virality scores for measured analytics or invent results for missing fields. When borrowing benchmarks, name the population, date range, paid/organic context, objective, and success definition. Ad spend, ROAS, revenue, attention, comprehension, and visual quality are distinct outcomes.

Keep design review alongside performance analysis: explain UX/communication strengths and friction, UI/craft strengths and improvements, and which audience-response claims require testing. Do not recommend trading meaning, attribution, accessibility, or brand fit for a metric.

## Retain the useful result

Update the active project's existing reference library with the asset, hypothesis, changed/fixed elements, actual outcome, limitations, feedback, and next decision. Label a brand preference, an observed result, and a performance hypothesis separately. Promote successful recipes with their applicable audience and conditions; do not silently generalize across brands.
