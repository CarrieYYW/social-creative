# Learn from References and Build Reusable Formats

Use for a supplied example, a collection of references, or research into relevant accounts. Scale the work to the question. One reference can support a useful craft observation; a larger dataset may support a pattern hypothesis. Neither establishes a causal explanation of success by itself.

## Establish evidence and relevance

Record each source, review date, inspection coverage, audience, format, and intended takeaway. Distinguish full playback, sampled frames, transcript, public description, and unavailable material. A sales page establishes what a vendor claims; it does not establish the quality or performance of an unseen edit. Keep missing material pending.

Start with the active account's existing work and available results when relevant. For external examples, consider audience overlap and different levels of visibility rather than collecting only viral outliers. Collect enough material to answer the question; do not require hundreds of posts for an ordinary reference review. Use available tools within the requested access and scope.

Record only supplied or accessible metrics, with their definitions and context. Public likes and views do not reveal private saves, audience comprehension, conversion, production cost, or paid distribution. Do not invent missing data or treat follower-normalized engagement as a complete comparison.

## Separate topic, format, and treatment

The topic is what the piece says; the format is how the idea unfolds; the treatment is how it looks and sounds. Extract the reusable structure without copying distinctive wording or another brand's identity.

For each useful pattern, explain:

- What was actually observed, using a slide/frame or timecode where available.
- What communication or production purpose it serves.
- Why it might fit this audience and what needs adaptation.
- What remains an audience-response hypothesis or an unsupported source claim.
- What should not transfer, and why.

For visual work, evaluate UX/communication and UI/craft explicitly. Static analysis should inspect hierarchy, reading order, progression, typography, spacing, contrast, and imagery. Video analysis should connect spoken words, display text, visual action, sound, and beat progression. Use the relevant production skill when available for detailed craft review; a strategy summary must not claim unperformed visual or playback inspection.

## Make the pattern usable

Store a format card in the project's existing reference library when useful:

| Field | What it captures |
|---|---|
| Purpose and audience | Intended takeaway, response, and who it serves |
| Opening promise | What makes the subject or value clear |
| Progression and resolution | How the idea develops and fulfills the promise |
| Treatment | Visual hierarchy, editorial rhythm, sound, or imagery that serves it |
| Production needs | Source copy, assets, filming, diagrams, or animation |
| Sources and limits | References, inspection coverage, unsuitable contexts, and untested claims |
| Outcomes | Actual artifacts, feedback, results, and conditions when available |

Choose duration, slide count, pace, and any CTA from the message and brand. Quiet or reflective work can have a clear opening and satisfying resolution without sensational language, constant motion, or manufactured urgency. Never fabricate a quote, testimonial, comment, result, or personal experience to fill a format.

Translate the card into approved copy and a representative visual or sequence. For production handoffs, provide the intended takeaway, source integrity requirements, brand context, beat/slide roles, and needed assets. A plan is not a finished visual or rendered video.

## Evaluate and retain

Use [Creative Testing and Learning](creative-testing.md) when comparing variants or interpreting performance. Adapt the pattern as a hypothesis before calling it proven for the account. Preserve the original/control and record meaningful changed/fixed elements.

Keep brand-specific taste and actual outcomes with the project. Retain general findings, source caveats, rejected approaches, and unreviewed links in [Creative research and learnings](creative-research.md). That notebook owns source provenance; the production skills own maintained working instructions.
