## Transferable observations and hypotheses

### Give an abstract idea a visible object or situation

A leaf makes monetary value concrete; a waiting place makes patience visible; a flower at ground level makes noticing physical. The practical lesson is to ask what a person would be doing when this tension appears. Start a brief with that action rather than attaching unrelated scenery after writing advice.

Hypothesis to evaluate: the situation can make an abstract reflection easier to recognize and remember. This study does not measure comprehension or attribute views to the technique.

### Use a relationship to carry a perspective

The selected examples repeatedly feature two friends, wide establishing shots, individual speaking/reaction shots and a shared environment. A second person provides someone to question, misunderstand, notice or respond. That is a reusable story role; another brand need not copy the actors, catchphrases, yellow type or locations.

Do not assume every second character must be wrong or needs a lesson. Write a plausible tension, keep dignity on both sides and let a changed action carry the ending where possible.

### Give the opening question a development and return

The pricing example moves from the large question to a small tangible object and returns to the larger question. That is a specific candidate structure: question → concrete test → changed context → return. It is more useful for planning than a generic instruction to be inspirational.

The waiting example also demonstrates why a blanket ban on greetings is too crude: a brief in-scene greeting can coexist with an immediate visual situation and title. Its effect on retention is unknown. Distinguish this from an empty channel introduction that delays the subject.

### Change shot scale when meaning changes

Observed visual vocabulary includes two-person wides for context/relationship, close-ups for a speaker or reaction, object inserts for the example and landscape/galaxy imagery for a larger perspective. The role of each shot matters more than a mandatory cut interval. The words example visibly expands image scale before returning to a person; this is an observed sequence, not proof of a full coherent audiovisual treatment.

### Treat sharing as an interpersonal use

Captions repeatedly address a friend or invite an answer. A useful planning question is: what might a person be communicating to someone by sending this? Affection, recognition, a shared habit or an invitation are candidate answers. This is not evidence that the caption or sentiment caused shares; do not add compulsory tagging prompts to every brand's posts.

### Preserve room for disagreement

The hiking comments included both recognition of perpetual striving and a reminder that some circumstances really can improve. Keep a modest message: noticing something valuable now need not invalidate ambition, difficult circumstances or needed change. Avoid turning an illustrative scene into universal mental-health advice.

## Candidate planning cards

These are original structures to test, not copied scripts or working video templates.

| Pattern | Brief structure | Useful when | Check before production |
|---|---|---|---|
| An idea made physical | Emotional tension → ordinary action/object → consequence → small different choice | Attention, connection, perfectionism, everyday values | Does the action communicate the tension without explanatory captions doing all the work? |
| Question, object, return | Simple question → concrete example → changed context → revisit question | Abstract values, perspective, reflective education | Does the example actually support the question, and is the ending earned? |
| Two plausible perspectives | Shared situation → different interpretations → response/choice that changes the interaction | Friendship, support, competing needs | Does either character become a straw person? Can the viewer reasonably disagree? |
| Near to wide, back to human | Specific object/person → wider relationship/context → personal response | Interdependence, attention, a researched explanation | Are the facts and imagery accurate/cleared? Is the scale change meaningful? |

For each idea retain: audience/use occasion; tension in one sentence; observable action; opening question/image; beat changes; ending image or choice; what someone might privately take from/share through it; feasible actors/assets/location; uncertainty and what to check. Choose duration from the actual scene. Do not make every idea fit one rigid number of beats.

Create variants from the same tension, not random topic lists: one scene, one sequential carousel and one standalone original thought. A carousel must carry a readable progression; a quote should stand alone and must not be attributed to the reference creator. No fabricated founder/customer anecdotes or therapeutic promises.

## Communication and visual craft review

**UX/communication:** the visible situations and questions give concrete entry points, and repeated characters provide a recognizable relationship. Full comprehension, pacing and payoff quality cannot be passed from sampled frames. Potential risk to test: a philosophical statement can overgeneralize or turn one friend into a lecturer; evaluate a complete script and full export.

**UI/craft:** the inspected samples use consistent yellow serif display/subtitle roles, dark edging on dialogue and shot-scale variation. These observations do not establish the exact font, grade, audio treatment, reading-time sufficiency or contrast across every frame. For another brand, retain its typography and verify phone-size text, safe areas and actual moving backgrounds. Sound remains unreviewed.
