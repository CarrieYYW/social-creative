# Brand Context for Social Production

The brand brief belongs with the active project and is shared by content planning, static design, and video work. Prefer an existing brand guide or brief over introducing a new store. If no appropriate document exists and the work warrants one, create a concise project-local `brand-brief.md` from known information; mark unknowns rather than inventing them.

When deriving context from a website, account, or reference, preserve the source/date for important facts and distinguish observed claims, inferred preferences, and unresolved questions. A site can establish its stated offer, not verified demand or permission to invent customer proof. Resolve conflicting prices, audience descriptions, or claims when they affect the work; leave unverified material labeled. Explicit project instructions take precedence over inferred taste.

Capture only what changes production decisions:

- **Identity and audience:** which brand/account, who the content is for, and relevant audience context.
- **Message and purpose:** the topic, intended takeaway, desired response, and source of the copy or claims.
- **Voice:** examples of suitable language, emotional tone, and relevant wording to avoid.
- **Visual language:** typography, colors, logos, imagery, composition, and existing templates or design-system sources.
- **Editorial language:** for video, pacing, caption treatment, music/sound character, motion, and examples that demonstrate the desired feeling.
- **References and feedback:** artifact links, what works, rejected treatments and why, and whether a note is a preference, convention, observation, or hypothesis.
- **Production context:** supplied assets, destination formats, existing editor, requested editable source, delivery location, and any real constraints on time or generation cost.

Separate reusable brand conventions from a one-off experiment. A treatment selected for one topic or campaign does not establish a brand-wide rule. When changing brands, load the new brand's sources and reassess references, tone, and production choices rather than carrying the previous project's taste forward.

For a coordinated set, the creative roles should read and update this same brief and refer to the same source copy and approved artifacts. Social-marketer can establish project research and strategy; social-content can prepare copy and calendars. The static designer owns slide composition and export; the video editor owns timeline, sound, motion, and playback verification. Each remains independently usable for a simple deliverable.
