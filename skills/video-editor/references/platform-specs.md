# Destination and Export Verification

Platform limits, upload surfaces, preview crops, and recommendations change. Do not rely on remembered duration caps, fixed safe-zone coordinates, or a single universal export preset.

## Establish the delivery contract

Identify the exact destination and surface: for example, an organic reel, a story, a short, a long-form video, or an ad placement. Record the intended aspect ratio, duration, resolution, frame rate, codec/container, audio needs, captions, cover, and editable-source requirements as relevant to that delivery.

Use current official platform guidance for technical limits and placement requirements. Prefer documentation from the destination itself, such as YouTube Help, Instagram/Meta Help or Business Help, and TikTok Support or Business Help. Advertising specifications do not automatically establish organic-post requirements. Record source links and the date checked with project export notes; do not hardcode a moving specification into the generic skill.

For example, [YouTube's Shorts eligibility guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en) demonstrates why an old generic duration limit is unsafe. Consult the current page for the requested upload rather than copying its limits into every brief.

If the destination is unknown or current guidance cannot be verified, produce a clearly labeled review export using stated settings and disclose the unverified requirements. A technically valid file is not proof it will pass a specific uploader.

## Compose for the actual surface

Check full-frame playback and relevant feed/profile/cover previews separately. Keep essential text, faces, products, and attribution clear of destination controls and likely crops. Recompose when changing ratios; do not assume a crop preserves the message.

Set frame rate with awareness of the source footage and motion. Inspect conversions for judder, dropped/duplicated frames, or interpolation defects. Check text and thin lines after compression. A higher export resolution does not repair an inadequate source.

## Verify the file

Inspect the rendered file's duration, dimensions, frame rate, video/audio streams, and codec/container against the delivery contract. Use available media inspection tools and record actual values rather than copying the intended settings into the report.

Play the export when possible; check the beginning, transitions, dense information, and ending as part of the full review. Verify caption timing, audio/video sync, clipping, missing media, and unintended black or frozen segments. Separate metadata inspection, sampled-frame review, and full playback in the completion report.

For multiple destinations, name the exports clearly and verify each one. Keep an editable source and the settings needed to reproduce it when the chosen tools support that handoff.
