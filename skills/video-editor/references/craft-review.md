# Review a substantial edit

Start with the viewer, the intended experience and the strongest piece of evidence in the source. Set two or three observable checks specific to the piece. For a tutorial, for example, a first-time viewer should see the control, its operation, and the result long enough to recognize it. For an interview, the viewer should understand the speaker’s claim and any qualification that changes its meaning.

## Test the hardest beat

Choose the part most likely to fail, such as a product action obscured by a vertical crop or a dense caption competing with an animated callout. Build the opening, hardest beat and payoff frames with actual media, type and copy. Play the hardest passage at delivery size, first with sound and then muted when the placement calls for it. Use what you see to decide whether to extend that treatment to the rest of the video.

Specify movement another editor can reproduce. Name the focal element, cue, start and end frames, settling position, readable hold and relationship to sound. A static proof can establish composition. Verify rhythm, synchronization and readability at playback speed.

## Review the encoded version

| Pass | What to look for | A concrete repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story and evidence | Can the viewer tell what happened and why it matters? Can every quoted line, demonstration and comparison be traced to the supplied material? | Restore the qualification or hold the actual result before introducing the next claim. |
| Picture, type and motion | Where does the eye land when the shot changes? Can the viewer read the densest caption at phone size while following the action? | Reframe the subject, shorten the editorial title, or delay the competing movement. Preserve spoken caption wording. |
| Sound and finish | Do cuts preserve intelligible speech, plausible ambience and deliberate pauses? Do adjacent shots match well enough to follow the action? | Restore the tail of a word, duck the music for the line, or adjust the mismatched shot. |
| Delivery | Does the exported file play to the intended end? Can the saved project reproduce the revision? | Reopen the saved project, correct the timing or missing asset and inspect the new encode. |

Preserve the first export when comparing treatments. Record the version, timestamp, observed defect and result of the repair. Review the changed passage and adjacent cuts, then watch the final assembly. Mark visual or listening checks that could not be performed. For intentional silence, record that choice and verify the applicable sound-off experience.

For a broad design rejection, revisit the source selection, framing and type hierarchy before polishing transitions. If two repairs leave the same problem, identify whether the constraint is the source, treatment or tool and try a different construction. Keep human acceptance and audience performance separate from the editor’s review. Record feedback against the exact version and scope; carry a preference into future work only when it applies to the new brief.
