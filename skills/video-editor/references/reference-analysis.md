# Video References and Production Recipes

Use when learning from a reference video, planning a repeatable treatment, or preparing a substantial edit with supporting footage. Scale the detail to the task; a caption correction does not need a teardown or shot list.

## Establish what was inspected

Record the source, inspection date, audience/goal, and coverage: full playback, sampled frames, transcript, public description, or unavailable. A transcript supports wording and broad structure; it cannot verify visual rhythm or sound. Sampled frames do not establish motion continuity. Keep inaccessible videos pending rather than reconstructing their edit from a sales page.

For the portions actually inspected, capture meaningful beats:

| Timecode | Spoken content | Display text / captions | Visual action | Sound | Editorial role / transferable move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Look at the relationship between the opening frame, spoken opening, and display text, plus how the film fulfills that promise. Inspect transitions in thought, demonstrations, pauses, image changes, music, and the ending or loop. Explain strengths and improvements in UX/communication and UI/craft separately, with timecoded evidence. Claims about retention or audience response remain hypotheses without actual analytics.

## Extract a treatment, not a duplicate

Identify what to borrow, why it fits this audience, what to adapt, and what would conflict with the brand or message. An engaging opening may be quiet, specific, or visually explanatory. Do not require constant motion, emotional escalation, a cut at fixed intervals, or an obligatory CTA.

Store a reusable format card in the project's existing reference library when useful: purpose/audience, opening promise, beat progression, resolution, visual/editorial language, sound approach, asset needs, source examples, and unsuitable contexts. Keep observed outcomes distinct from taste and predicted performance.

## Convert the treatment into a production plan

For work that needs planning, map actual source material or approved copy to beats:

| Beat / target time | Source in/out or approved words | On-screen information | Visual/action and asset source | Sound / transition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use provisional timing before recording and real voice/footage timing when available. Mark absent assets, sourcing/generation needs, and unresolved coverage. Supporting visuals should demonstrate, explain, establish context, provide evidence, express atmosphere, or bridge an edit. Retain the speaker when expression matters. Avoid unrelated stock footage that merely matches a noun.

For filming, convert coverage gaps into a practical shot list: subject/action, framing/angle, approximate duration, beat served, essential/optional status, and an available fallback. Group filming by location or setup to reduce repeated work. For animation, describe what changes over time and what the change communicates; disconnected attractive poses are not a complete motion plan.

Keep the plan proportional to the request. Resolve story and broad timing in the rough cut before costly finishing. A beat sheet, storyboard, or shot list is a production artifact, not proof of a finished render; inspect the actual export using the main skill's playback, sound, caption, and delivery checks.

## Revise and learn

Preserve the original/edit recipe. When testing a revision, state the hypothesis and changed/fixed elements. Changing the first frame, spoken line, and display text together tests an opening package, not each element separately. Keep the rest stable where practical. Treat non-random organic comparisons as directional and do not promise a result without analytics.

Record the actual outcome, source asset, version, applicable context, limitations, and next decision in the project reference library. A viewer drop-off suggests a place to inspect; it does not prove the cause. Content strategy and analytics can use social-content when available without making it a prerequisite for an ordinary edit.
