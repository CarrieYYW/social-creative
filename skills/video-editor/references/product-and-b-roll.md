# B-roll, supplemental visuals and product animation

## Decide why a new visual is needed

Assign each insert a job: show actual proof, explain a mechanism, establish context, compress time, show a detail/reaction, support emotion, or bridge an honest editorial cut. Avoid random stock footage used only to keep the screen changing. Sometimes the original face/action is the best picture.

For each insert, specify output interval, linked spoken idea, intended viewer inference, visual brief, aspect ratio/crop, source method, continuity anchors, required duration/handles, sound relation, usage scope, inspection status and implementation status. Preserve underlying dialogue when a cutaway supports it. Sound effects should correspond to the intended action or be clearly stylistic.

## Choose a production route

| Need | Preferred route | Check before use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual product behavior/result | Supplied demo, new authorized screen recording, or filmed action | Real function, version, data and result; readable evidence |
| Physical product beauty/detail | Supplied accurate product photos/footage or a verified model | Geometry, materials, logo, proportions, color and continuity |
| Explanation of a concept/process | Designed diagram, infographic or editable motion graphic | Accurate relationships/data and enough reading time |
| Context or atmosphere | Suitable owned footage, licensed stock, or generated illustration | Relevance, consistent visual treatment and permitted use |
| Cut coverage | Alternate angle, detail/reaction, or purposeful graphic | No misleading implication about chronology or response |

Use available assets before spending on replacements. If generated imagery/video is needed, use an appropriate available tool and its workflow. Produce the asset, inspect it, then integrate it. A prompt, stock-search suggestion or placeholder is only a production plan. If a tool, asset or budget prevents creation, leave the requirement explicit and complete other work; do not silently claim fulfillment.

## B-roll direction and integration

Give a shot brief with subject, exact action, composition/shot size, setting, light direction, camera movement, duration, entry/exit handles, aesthetic and prohibited inaccuracies. Limit competing actions so the shot has a clear focal point. Match adjacent scene direction and mood, or establish an intentional transition.

Inspect chosen footage for useful start/middle/end frames, legibility, identity/object consistency, text/logos, artifacts, stabilization, movement and audio. Avoid upscaling a tiny crop as a substitute for quality. Match grade, texture and sound space across sources. Let a useful proof insert play long enough to be understood. Mark fictional/generated illustrations when needed to avoid suggesting they document real people/events/results.

## Build a purposeful insert sequence

Turn each line or story beat into a visible question: what should the viewer now see or understand that the main shot does not show? Select the strongest available answer, not the first clip matching a keyword. A line about craftsmanship may need a real process or material detail; a generic workshop exterior does not establish how a product was made. If no asset proves a claim, rewrite the claim, use clearly identified illustration, or leave that proof requirement unresolved.

Create a compact selects table for the chosen passage: beat/meaning, source ID and verified usable range, visible action/detail, entry/exit handles, output interval, crop/focal point, underlying audio, return shot and reason to keep it. Distinguish candidate ranges from inspected selects. Inspect the entire selected range for an action that develops, consistency and artifacts; endpoints alone can miss a mutation. Retain enough source before/after the cut for the chosen transition. Use the source's actual duration rather than assuming a stock or generated clip has usable handles.

Example sequence for a fictional bowl study: **overall form → rim/material detail → bowl in its setting**. The second image should reveal something unavailable in the first; the last restores scale and context. This is different from repeating three near-identical beauty shots. Generated texture may illustrate an aesthetic, but cannot prove handmade manufacture, durability or a real product specification. For an authentic tutorial, the corresponding progression might be **relevant control → recorded action → legible result**, preserving the real operation and result timing. For an interview, a held face may be more revealing than a cutaway: do not insert atmosphere over the decisive reaction merely to increase visual variety.

Choose the relationship into and out of the insert. Use a clean cut, matched focal position, split audio edit or a designed transition from [motion craft](motion-craft.md). Keep narration and captions aligned through the cutaway; resume visible speech in sync. B-roll does not automatically require a caption redesign or a sound effect. Give the new image time to establish and the relevant action time to finish. Check its actual crop together with captions and platform UI clearance. If the insert's useful detail is lost at delivery size, recompose or replace it.

Judge the assembled passage both with the insert and with the simpler primary-shot edit. Keep the insert when it adds evidence, explanation, emotion or necessary coverage. If it merely restates the words decoratively, shorten it, replace it or remove it. Record this as an editorial judgment, not a technical pass or predicted engagement gain.

## Product animation workflow

1. Identify the product and animation's purpose: reveal, demonstrate, explain a feature, show an interaction, compare states or emphasize a detail.
2. Inventory trusted inputs: actual UI captures, product images, vectors, CAD/3D model, measurements, brand assets and verified feature behavior. Separate documented facts from assumptions.
3. Choose the lowest-complexity method that can faithfully communicate the idea. A photo cutout plus lighting/typography or a real UI close-up may be better than an invented 3D object.
4. Design hero, detail/proof and payoff frames; define camera, object hierarchy, material/light treatment, callouts and final readable state.
5. Use [motion craft](motion-craft.md) to specify frame intervals, property values, pivots, curves, masks, holds, transitions and sound cues. Keep editable elements where practical.
6. Produce the sequence with the available renderer/compositor or generation tool. Review product accuracy and motion before inserting it into the short.

For digital products, animate actual recorded actions or clearly labeled conceptual mockups. Keep cursor interactions, loading states and result timing truthful. Never invent working controls or show private data. Crop toward relevant evidence rather than shrinking an entire desktop into a phone frame.

For physical products, preserve geometry, dimensions, logo and materials. Do not spin a single flat photograph to reveal nonexistent back/side surfaces; request more views, use a verified model, or choose a 2D/2.5D treatment within visible information. Exploded views and cutaways require verified parts/structure or an explicit conceptual label. Never depict unsupported physical performance as proof.

When the user means general produced animation rather than a physical/digital product, route to explanatory graphics, kinetic type, diagrams, abstract motifs or character/scene animation as appropriate; do not unnecessarily restrict the workflow to product advertising.

## Acceptance

Verify product identity, actual capabilities, geometry/UI text, brand consistency, readable details, motion continuity, crop, timing, audio and artifact-free selected frames. Confirm source permissions and any required disclosure for the chosen use. Check the asset in the final composite, including overlaps with captions, rather than judging it only in isolation. Keep native/editable source with any pre-rendered insert and record the asset version in the final edit plan.
