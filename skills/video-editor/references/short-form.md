# Reels and Short Video

## Choose the structure from the content

Identify what the viewer should understand or feel, why they should stay, and how the piece resolves. Choose length from the idea and source material, then check the destination's requirements. Do not force a fixed duration, a sensational hook, a CTA, or a cut every few seconds onto every brand.

## Animated quotes

Preserve exact quotation and attribution. Distinguish original copy, paraphrase, and excerpt. Break the text at natural thought boundaries and allow time to read each phrase at the intended display size. Avoid word-by-word animation that makes a contemplative passage feel frantic or obscures its complete meaning.

Use motion with a purpose: reveal a phrase, change emphasis, shift attention, or connect two ideas. Treat text as controlled typography rather than asking a video model to regenerate it. Inspect holds, transitions, and loop boundaries; an elegant still does not guarantee readable motion.

## Visual explainers

Build a short argument or demonstration: the question, the mechanism or relationship, a concrete example, and a resolution as appropriate. Map each beat to something the viewer can learn from seeing it. Prefer diagrams, demonstrations, and meaningful metaphors over decorative stock clips that merely echo individual nouns.

Plan narration and on-screen information together. Do not make the viewer read a dense paragraph while listening to different dense speech. Keep recurring objects, labels, and colors consistent. When no narration is requested, ensure the visual sequence can carry the explanation itself.

## Talking head with supporting visuals

Review the source and mark candidate passages with in/out timecodes. Select a complete thought that works without the omitted conversation; preserve qualifications and context. Remove repetition or dead time when it improves the story, while retaining pauses that carry meaning.

Give every supporting image, clip, or animation a role: demonstrate, explain, establish context, provide evidence, or bridge a necessary edit. Keep the speaker visible when their expression or delivery matters. Reframe around the active subject and actual overlay zones rather than automatically center-cropping every shot.

## Caption and visual design

Set a readable caption region that avoids faces, demonstrations, and destination UI. Check actual line breaks, contrast against changing footage, and hold time. Use emphasis sparingly and avoid animations that compete with speech. Review automatically generated captions against the audio, especially names, quotations, and topic-specific terms.

Use a consistent type hierarchy for captions, headings, labels, and attribution. Build or use a representative frame to set the visual language before extending it. Compare several moments together to catch drift in image treatment, typography, and graphics.

## Rough cut, refinement, and delivery

For a substantial piece, settle message, sequence, and broad timing in the rough cut before polishing every transition. For a small edit, work directly on the requested change. Keep what is fixed clear when responding to feedback.

Review the whole sequence for message and rhythm, then inspect difficult moments: opening frame, dense captions, subject changes, cuts, animation extremes, and ending/loop. Verify sound and sound-off viewing where relevant. Deliver a cover, caption file, or alternate crop when requested or needed for the agreed destination; avoid multiplying deliverables without a reason.
