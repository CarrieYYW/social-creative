# Generating Footage within an Edit

Select generation tools for a concrete shot requirement and the capabilities actually available. Model versions, controls, duration limits, pricing, and access change; verify the relevant official documentation before recommending a service, quoting limits, or designing a tool-specific workflow.

## Design the shot first

Specify the subject, action, composition, environment, lighting, image treatment, intended movement, continuity constraints, and editorial role. Use a clear prompt describing the desired result. More adjectives do not substitute for a decision about the shot.

Explore low-cost representative frames when the direction is unresolved. Once selected, use reference images, character/object sheets, or the model's supported consistency controls to derive related shots. Confirm that a feature is supported by the chosen model/version. A seed or repeated prompt alone does not guarantee identity or style consistency; see [Midjourney's seed guidance](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32604356340877-Seeds) for a concrete example of that distinction.

For an image-to-video shot, establish a strong still and describe what should move and what should remain stable. For a multi-shot sequence, plan the key frames or storyboard before generating all motion. Use existing footage, compositing, or controlled graphics when those offer the precision the shot needs.

## Preserve critical content

Keep exact text, logos, diagrams, and product details in controlled source layers where feasible. Verify any generated rendition against the approved source. Check identity, proportions, materials, lighting, camera direction, and screen position across shots.

Follow the host's image/video-generation tool instructions and inspect referenced assets before editing them. Verify available credits, costs, and user constraints before initiating substantial paid generation. Keep source references, model/version, parameters, and selected results in the project so the process can be reproduced as far as the tool permits.

## Judge the sequence

Inspect motion throughout each selected clip, including faces, hands, object integrity, physics, camera movement, and transitions. Check continuity in the assembled edit, not just attractive isolated frames. Repair, regenerate, trim, or replace defects according to their effect on the story.

Color, texture, grain, sharpness, and sound should make generated material belong with existing footage. Do not conceal substantive product or factual inaccuracies under a visual grade. Report generation failures or unavailable capabilities honestly; a prompt is not a produced clip.
