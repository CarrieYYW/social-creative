# Sound for Video

## Start by listening

Identify the role of speech, music, ambience, effects, and silence. Inspect the source for intelligibility, noise, distortion, room changes, and sync issues. Preserve the original recordings and work on an editable mix or reproducible processing chain.

Treat narration and dialogue as the reference when the message depends on speech. Apply noise reduction, EQ, de-essing, compression, or limiting to solve audible problems rather than copying a universal preset. Compare processed and original audio at comparable listening levels; aggressive cleanup can damage the voice.

## Build the sound arc

For repeatable work, keep a project-specific sound palette with asset paths, source/rights context, suitable uses, and alternatives. Add purposeful sound cues to the existing beat plan rather than maintaining a disconnected effects schedule. Read [Project Visual and Sound Treatment](project-treatment.md) when building that guide. Recheck cues against the edited narration timeline after cuts or voice changes.

Choose music for the piece's emotional movement and brand. It can build, recede, or be absent. Avoid competing vocals or frequencies under important speech; automate or duck the bed as the actual mix requires. Fixed fader values do not establish intelligibility or measured loudness.

Preserve useful pauses and room tone at edits. Use ambience and sound effects to support a visible action, setting, transition, or idea. Silence and deliberately noticeable sound can both be appropriate; judge them in context rather than applying an absolute rule.

Use supplied, original, or appropriately licensed material. Check the actual asset and destination terms when sourcing; a service subscription or a platform's music library does not imply unrestricted reuse across brands, placements, or platforms.

## Mix and measure

Set delivery loudness and true-peak requirements from the agreed destination and current official specifications where applicable. Distinguish measured integrated loudness and true peak from track gain or sample peak. Do not present a remembered platform normalization target as a mandatory delivery standard.

Listen through the assembled edit for masking, clipping, clicks, abrupt noise-floor changes, unwanted silence, inconsistent voice levels, and audio/video drift. Check mono compatibility and small-speaker intelligibility when those matter to the destination. Use the available monitoring environment and disclose its limits rather than claiming a physical-device check that was not performed.

Review the exported file as well as the working timeline. Codec changes, edits, and export settings can introduce problems that were absent in the source mix. Keep voice, music, and effect sources or stems when the requested editable handoff supports them.
