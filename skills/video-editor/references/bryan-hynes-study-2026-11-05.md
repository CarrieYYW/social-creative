## Text is a system of roles

The observed reels do not use one font treatment everywhere:

- **Opening display:** the starting-late opening uses warm yellow, very heavy rounded display lettering, a smaller setup line and one substantially larger emphasized word. The whole block is compact near the middle of the frame. Later examples reduce to a bold name and a smaller contextual line in a consistent zone. Exact font identity is unknown.
- **Narrative captions:** the reflective reel places small white sans-serif phrases near mid-frame with minimal visual competition. The imagery retains prominence. This is a useful role distinction, not evidence that those captions are readable for every viewer; phone-size testing and an accessible caption route are still needed.
- **Instructional labels:** the hiking breakdown puts a bold white shot label and a smaller explanatory line high over comparatively quiet sky. Placement relates to the moving subject and available image area. The opening has a much larger stacked title with a textured/image-like fill; its exact matte construction was not verified.
- **Editorial serif:** the solo mountain result uses an all-caps white serif headline with a smaller serif explanation. Serif is one chosen role here, not the sole definition of a cinematic treatment.

For production, define title, supporting line, instruction and caption styles independently. Keep line breaks, relative scale, maximum width, alignment, background contrast, time in/out and affected scene explicit. Anchor text to stable composition zones, then inspect it against each shot. Repeated small captions need deliberate readability checks, especially over bright or detailed imagery. Implement complete thought groups where appropriate; exact animation timing must be studied before reproducing it.

## Candidate patterns to build and compare

1. **Connected everyday sequence with restrained captions.** Own footage of one small action, meaningful coverage changes, a visual resolution, and an editable phrase track. For a contemplative brand, a possible original sequence is putting the phone aside → opening a notebook → writing → stepping outside. This is our proposed content, not his script. Test whether the footage carries the idea with the text hidden.
2. **Display opening → compact recurring labels.** Separate opening hierarchy from later information. Parameters: text, font choices, relative scale, line breaks, warm/neutral color choice, placement, per-beat holds and independent in/out behavior. Start from our content and type choices; do not copy his catchphrases or claim the exact font/preset.
3. **Finished sequence → optional teaching overlay.** Keep the same cut playable clean or with shot/action labels. Parameters: label copy, scene mapping, label placement and overlay mode. This preserves the sequence while making the method explainable.

For static quotes/carousels, test the display/support relationship, image-led composition and stable reading zones with original copy. A strong still needs its own spacing and full readable thought; do not freeze an arbitrary subtitle moment. Alternate quote lengths and images are required before calling a layout reusable.

These candidates need editable source, example inputs/media, rendered comparisons, exact reproduction steps and user feedback before promotion. No Bryan-specific font, color, music or caption position is installed as a general plugin default.
