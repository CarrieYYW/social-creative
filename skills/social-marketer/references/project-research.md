# Project Social Marketing Research

Use for a new project's foundation, a meaningful campaign decision, or an account/strategy audit. Reuse prior research and refresh only what may change the recommendation. The deliverable is a supported decision, not a fixed quantity of sources or an exhaustive scrape.

## Frame the question

Establish the brand/account, audience, intended benefit or offer, goal, stage, geography/language when relevant, production capacity, and decision to make. Read the current brand guide and business strategy first. For example, an education project may need better comprehension and returning audience; a local service may need qualified enquiries. Choose evidence and measures accordingly.

If the project is new, build a provisional foundation and identify what needs validation. If it is established, inspect existing material and outcomes before recommending a reset. Distinguish a messaging issue, distribution issue, production limitation, and missing measurement; none follows automatically from low views.

## Gather relevant evidence

| Evidence | Useful questions | Limits to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Project sources and approved claims | What is offered, to whom, with what proof and voice? | A website statement is not independent proof; conflicting or stale claims need reconciliation |
| Customer/audience feedback, enquiries, search and support questions | What do people need, misunderstand, value, or ask next? | Separate direct statements from interpretation; do not invent representative quotes |
| Available account analytics and posts | Which comparable topics/formats serve the stated goal? | Record definitions, distribution, elapsed time, paid promotion, sample limits, and missing fields |
| Relevant peers, alternatives, and public discussions | What messages and formats recur, and what useful gaps exist? | Visible engagement is incomplete; include ordinary work and counterexamples, not just viral outliers |
| Current official platform guidance | Which technical requirements or available analytics affect the decision? | Verify the actual surface, account context, and date; paid specifications may not apply to organic posts |

Find relevant sources rather than asking the user to supply an arbitrary competitor count. Use connected read tools and public browsing where useful; stay within the requested scope. Availability gaps should be explicit. Do not promise private insights without access or imply an account was connected because a public profile was read.

For video references, state whether the review used full playback, sampled frames, transcript, or a description. A transcript supports wording/structure but cannot establish motion or sound quality. For static sequences, name missing slides. Source quality and inspection coverage constrain the strength of the recommendation.

## Synthesize rather than collect

Use a compact evidence record when it helps:

| Question / finding | Source and date | Observed / inferred / hypothesis | Implication | Confidence / gaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Separate audience needs from our proposed response. Identify the strongest supporting evidence, a meaningful counterexample or uncertainty, and the decision it informs. Explain what this project can credibly contribute; a content gap does not establish willingness to buy or follow.

Translate research into a small set of priorities, with a reason to choose them over the relevant alternatives. Choose topic/pillar count, channel mix, format mix, and cadence from the evidence and capacity. Relevance and feasibility matter more than a generic industry schedule. State tradeoffs when pursuing reach might weaken comprehension, brand trust, or a useful next action.

## Retain a usable strategy

Reuse existing project docs. If the project needs new homes, an evidence record and a concise decision brief are usually enough. Include only fields that change work:

- Audience and desired outcome, with known facts and assumptions distinguished.
- Channel priorities and rationale, positioning/message, credible source/proof, topics and format directions.
- Capacity, production constraints, timing rationale, and a useful next action when appropriate.
- Goal-relevant measures, baseline, initial hypotheses/experiments, uncertainty, and review triggers.
- A production handoff: audience, takeaway, angle, source copy/proof, format, brand rules, and assets needed.

Keep the shared brand brief authoritative for brand facts. If drafting it from a site/account, preserve provenance and mark inferred preferences or unresolved claims. The decision brief can link to brand facts rather than repeat them. Research artefacts do not authorize publication, account access changes, outreach, or spend.

## Refresh from results

Review when the goal, offer, audience questions, channel context, or meaningful results change. Decide an observation window and primary metric before interpreting a test; record actual exposure and comparable baselines. Preserve original/control versions and account for timing, audience, paid distribution, and other confounders. A package test cannot isolate every element changed.

Report observations first, then plausible explanations and the next decision. Inconclusive is a valid result. Retain the asset/version, actual metrics, explicit feedback, applicability, and what remains untested. Update the strategy when evidence warrants it; ordinary production requests can continue from the existing foundation.
